# Oko Evidence Adjudication Protocol, v0.6.0

## Decision

The v0.3.0 Oko assessment classified six practical-control stages as `supported` while relying on retrospective participant accounts and an independent retrospective reconstruction. The governing practical-control test requires direct, contemporaneous evidence for that state. This adjudication resolves the mismatch without changing the v0.3.0 packet, assessment, protocol, or evidence cutoff.

## Question

Which practical-control states follow when the existing state definitions are applied to the frozen Oko source packet exactly as written?

## Frozen evidence universe

The adjudication uses only the Oko source manifest and case report frozen in v0.3.0. The evidence cutoff remains 6 August 2026 at 23:59:59 UTC. Sources O2, O3, and O4 may support the reconstructed sequence. Their publication dates are 1999, 2014, and 2017. The incident occurred in 1983. No located Soviet command log, official incident record, or investigation file corroborates the sequence contemporaneously.

No new historical source search enters this adjudication. A later source discovery requires a new source packet and a separate versioned decision.

## Fixed state rule

Apply the assessment states in `protocols/practical-human-control-test.md` without amendment:

- `supported`: direct, contemporaneous evidence satisfies every required element of the stage;
- `partially_supported`: the evidence satisfies at least one required element and a material requirement remains unmet;
- `unsupported`: eligible evidence contradicts the stage or shows the condition was absent;
- `indeterminate`: the packet lacks enough evidence to decide and does not satisfy a material element;
- `outside_scope`: the stage does not apply within the declared boundary.

Retrospective first-person testimony counts as direct evidence of what the participant later reported. It does not count as a contemporaneous record of the 1983 decision. Independent retrospective reconstruction may corroborate the reported sequence. It does not supply the missing contemporaneous record.

## Prespecified adjudication scope

Reassess these six Oko practical-control stages independently:

1. access;
2. comprehension;
3. authority;
4. feasibility;
5. exercise;
6. effect.

Correction and repair remain outside the bounded sequence. Reform retains its v0.3.0 state because the present mismatch does not concern the later investigation or institutional change. The autonomy profile and twelve trust-evidence findings remain frozen v0.3.0 outputs and receive no new validity claim.

## Stage decision procedure

For each stage:

1. identify the minimum evidence stated in the practical-control test;
2. list the frozen source propositions that address that requirement;
3. record whether the proposition is direct, contemporaneous, both, or neither;
4. identify the material missing element;
5. assign the state using the fixed rule;
6. write the narrowest proposition the evidence permits;
7. state the interpretation excluded by the missing evidence.

The adjudicator may not infer a contemporaneous record from agreement among later accounts. A known outcome may not supply an unrecorded intermediate control condition.

## Dependency rule

A bounded practical-force conclusion requires `supported` findings for access, authority, feasibility, exercise, and effect. Any `partially_supported`, `unsupported`, or `indeterminate` finding in that required chain makes the conclusion ineligible. The assessment may still report what the retrospective sources describe.

## Failure conditions
