# v0.17.0: Policy crosswalk and arXiv-ready preprint

## Why this release matters

The paper argues that assigning a human role is not enough to prove practical human control. Institutions also need evidence that the person received usable information, could understand it, had authority and time to intervene, exercised judgment, and affected execution. Version 0.17.0 shows how selected public policy and standards sources relate to those six questions without treating a policy requirement as proof that control worked in a specific event.

## What was added

- A bounded official-source review covering EU AI Act Articles 14 and 57, selected NIST AI RMF functions, the public ISO/IEC 42001 overview, and selected United States oversight rules.
- A crosswalk from those sources to the six-stage practical-control chain.
- A prospective EU AI Act Article 57 regulatory-sandbox design for testing the method on contemporary evidence.
- Five new claim-evidence records, eight official-source records, one author-review attestation, and nine controlled mutations.
- An updated 30-page v0.17.0 preprint, deterministic 12-member arXiv source archive, source manifest, compile receipt, citation metadata, and reviewer guide.

## Integrity result

All nine prespecified policy-crosswalk corruptions were detected. The checks test whether source identities, scope labels, evidence links, claim permissions, and method-output relationships remain consistent. They do not determine whether a law has been interpreted conclusively or whether an institution complies with it.

## Formatting result

The exact source compiles successfully in Overleaf with XeLaTeX. The final PDF uses the approved single-column navy design, keeps all displays before References, labels the work as a preprint, and formats references alphabetically with no bullets and a 0.5-inch hanging indent.

## Preserved findings and limits

The historical case findings do not change: Oko remains unresolved, both Patriot cases fail the complete event-control rule, and none of the three selected cases passes. The cases remain purposive, retrospective, and assessed by one author. This release makes no prevalence, causal-effect, legal-compliance, ISO-conformity, policy-effectiveness, contemporary-transfer, or publication-acceptance claim.
