# v0.16.0: Practical Human Control Manuscript Rebuild

## Decision

This release rebuilds the paper around the question an institutional reader must answer: does a named human reviewer have practical power over one consequential automated decision? It preserves the released case states and search counts, then makes the case-level rule and its consequences explicit.

## Why the revision matters

A role assignment shows who carries responsibility on paper. It does not show that the person received usable information, understood it, could intervene in time, exercised judgment, or changed execution. When an institution treats the role assignment as proof of control, it can count an ineffective safeguard as functioning and place responsibility on someone who lacked practical power.

Version 0.16.0 turns that broad concern into a connected documentary test. It also explains why each research control exists, what inference error it prevents, and which conclusion remains outside the evidence.

## Formal result

The event-control rule requires six supported stages:

1. information access;
2. comprehension capacity;
3. intervention authority;
4. intervention feasibility;
5. exercised judgment; and
6. execution propagation.

The rule returns `pass` when all six stages are supported, `fail` when any required stage is unsupported, and `unresolved` when no stage is unsupported but at least one is partially supported or indeterminate.

Applied to the released states:

- Oko is unresolved because all six required stages are partially supported.
- Patriot ZG710 fails because four required stages are unsupported.
- Patriot F/A-18C fails because execution propagation is unsupported and three other stages are indeterminate.
- No selected case passes.

These are results for three purposively selected public packets. They provide no prevalence, causal, reliability, safety, legal, or contemporary-system transfer estimate.

## Integrity result

The v0.16.0 Chain-of-Evidence audit returns `PASS_WITH_EXCEPTIONS`. Traceability, exact-value verification, reference resolution, method-code alignment, evidence fitness, dependency closure, and negative controls cover the new formal claims. This means the declared artifacts agree and the controls respond to seeded errors. It does not mean the historical interpretation is independently correct.
