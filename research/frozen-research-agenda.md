# Frozen Research Agenda

**Version:** 0.3.0  
**Freeze date:** 7 August 2026  
**Status:** Fixed for the next public-case research cycle

## Decision served

The next research cycle must determine whether the evidence architecture can produce useful, bounded conclusions about practical human control from public incident records. Three topics define that work. The order below is fixed for version 0.3.0 and may change only through a recorded release decision.

## Topic 1: Practical force of human authority

Formal review, approval, override, or stop authority can fail when information arrives late, decision windows are compressed, system explanations are weak, or organizational training discourages challenge. This topic examines the operating conditions required for assigned authority to affect an action before it becomes irreversible.

The primary mechanism is the interaction among access, comprehension, authority, feasibility, exercise, and effect. A control claim fails when any required link lacks sufficient support.

## Topic 2: Evidence sufficiency for incident reconstruction

Public reports often establish an outcome while leaving system state, operator information, causal mechanism, or corrective action unresolved. This topic asks which records support a bounded conclusion and which missing records require an indeterminate finding.

The primary mechanism is claim-to-source traceability. Each material statement must be marked as a direct record, a source claim, an assessor inference, or unresolved.

## Topic 3: Interactions among access, authority, time, and information independence

Controls that appear adequate in isolation may fail together. A person can possess authority without timely access. Multiple alerts can appear corroborative while sharing one source or one flawed classification. An available override can be unusable inside the actual decision window.

The primary mechanism is conjunctive control. Individual controls contribute to practical human control only when their interaction permits an informed intervention with downstream effect.

## Frozen project question

> **Under what operational conditions does formal human oversight have practical force in high-consequence AI-mediated decisions, and what public evidence is sufficient to justify that conclusion?**

## Evidence plan

The project will:

1. preserve frozen candidate collections and every screening decision;
2. publish source manifests that distinguish available, partial, and unavailable evidence;
3. apply the same trust-evidence and practical-control fields to each selected case;
4. preserve unsupported and indeterminate findings instead of imputing missing facts;
5. compare the cases at the level of mechanisms and evidence conditions;
6. avoid population, causal-effectiveness, legal, and general-safety claims.

## Change rule

A later release may revise a topic or the project question only if its changelog identifies the evidence that prompted the change, the affected claims, the migration effect on existing case packets, and the remaining uncertainty.
