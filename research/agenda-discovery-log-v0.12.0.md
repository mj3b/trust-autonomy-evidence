## Findings

### 1. Retrieval completion and screening completion are separate gates

All 102 selected records now have an outcome. Seventy-one have readable source content, yet none has an author decision in this tranche.

**Sequence effect:** Keep the 71 records outside manuscript claims until the author queue records a decision and rationale.

### 2. Public identifier resolution leaves a material access remainder

Twenty-six records yielded metadata without readable source content, and two remained unavailable. Purchase pages, automated access checks, legacy publisher failures, and source-title mismatches contributed to those states.

**Sequence effect:** Preserve these records as coverage limits. Later HOLLIS or authenticated-interface work may recover them. They cannot be converted into topic exclusions.

### 3. Version reconciliation is part of coverage control

Three sampled records resolved to duplicate versions of work already represented in the corpus.

**Sequence effect:** Use identifier and version matching before counting new evidence. Keep duplicate outcomes in the ledger so later reviewers can reconstruct the count.

### 4. Abstract recovery can be useful without granting full claim permission

Thirty-seven publisher or repository routes supplied abstracts. Those abstracts can support relevance screening and bounded descriptions of what a source declares.

**Sequence effect:** If the author retains one of these records, any manuscript proposition that exceeds the abstract requires a separate full-text check.

### 5. Solo research can close process gates without simulating independence

The retrieval ledger, source routes, queue, and validator make this pass reproducible by another person. They do not supply a second assessor.

**Sequence effect:** Continue the author-controlled evidence gates. Keep independent reliability in the unresolved-claims register until an unaffiliated review occurs.
