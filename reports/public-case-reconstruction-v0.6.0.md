## Oko adjudication

The v0.3.0 assessment classified all six Oko stages as supported. Its participant accounts directly recorded what Stanislav Petrov later reported. They were retrospective and could not satisfy the current rule requiring direct, contemporaneous evidence for a supported historical state.

The [v0.6.0 protocol](../protocols/oko-evidence-adjudication-v0.6.0.md) froze the evidence universe, cutoff, stage rules, and dependency rule before the reassessment. The [v0.6.0 assessment](../assessments/v0.6.0/TAE-PUB-001-oko-1983.json) classifies each stage as partially supported. The [change ledger](../assessments/v0.6.0/oko-change-ledger.json) records every transition and hashes the frozen v0.3.0 inputs. The older assessment remains unchanged.

## Central inference

Assigned authority does not determine the remaining practical-control states. Oko now carries partial support across the entire chain. ZG710 records supported authority with unsupported comprehension, feasibility, exercise, and effect. F/A-18C records supported authority with unresolved comprehension, feasibility, and exercise.

The mechanism requires separate evidence for information, understanding, permission, opportunity, action, and effect. The current three-case pattern is descriptive and purposefully selected. It supplies no prevalence, causal-effect, independent-reliability, or current-system transfer estimate.

## Institutional implication

An institution seeking to substantiate human control should preserve the information shown to the reviewer, timing, system state, independent evidence, authority, available interventions, action taken, and downstream effect. The record set is a proposed evidentiary requirement. Its presence does not guarantee a safe, compliant, or beneficial outcome.

## Remaining limits

- One assessor designed and applied the procedure.
- Oko relies on retrospective accounts and lacks located contemporaneous command records.
- The cases were selected to instantiate three declared strata.
- Two cases concern the same Patriot system family and operating period.
- Historical knowledge-based systems differ materially from current learned systems.
