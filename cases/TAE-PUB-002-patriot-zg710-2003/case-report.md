# TAE-PUB-002: Patriot Engagement of RAF Tornado ZG710

## Decision

On 22 March 2003, a U.S. Patriot battery near the Iraq-Kuwait border classified RAF Tornado ZG710 as an anti-radiation missile. The crew authorized an engagement that destroyed the aircraft and killed both aircrew members.

Formal authority was present. Practical control failed because operators lacked sufficient time, information, independent checks, and preparation to challenge the system's classification. The case therefore separates assigned authority from authority that can change an action under operating conditions.

## Boundary

The sequence begins when the battery detected and classified ZG710 and ends when the Patriot missile destroyed the aircraft. The later inquiry and reforms are included only to assess correction, repair, and reform.

## Assessment

The autonomy profile is narrow goal scope, limited execution authority, a multi-step horizon, public impact, pre-action oversight, and irreversible action. The full machine-readable determination appears in [`assessment.json`](assessment.json).

Access is partially supported and formal authority is supported. Comprehension, feasibility, exercise, and effect are unsupported. The interaction matters: a formal stop option has little practical force when the operator cannot form and execute an informed challenge before the action becomes irreversible.

## Supported proposition

Patriot operators retained formal engagement authority, yet incomplete information, a compressed decision window, training to trust the system, and the division of work between people and automation prevented that authority from functioning as an effective challenge before launch.

## Excluded conclusions

- The packet does not establish the mental state or culpability of any individual operator.
- The event does not establish the reliability of every Patriot configuration or deployment.
- The assessment does not determine legal responsibility or compliance with rules of engagement.
