# TAE-PUB-003: Patriot Engagement of a U.S. Navy F/A-18C

## Decision

On 2 April 2003, Patriot batteries detected and classified a track as a tactical ballistic missile. A command center correlated the reports, confidence increased, and an engagement order led to the destruction of a U.S. Navy F/A-18C and the death of its pilot.

The public record supports the action sequence. It does not disclose enough evidence to determine why the system misclassified the aircraft, whether the correlated reports were independent, what operators saw, or which interventions were feasible.

## Boundary

The sequence begins with the Patriot detections and ends when the battery destroyed the F/A-18C. The later investigation and reforms are included only to assess correction, repair, and reform.

## Assessment

The autonomy profile is narrow goal scope, limited execution authority, a multi-step horizon, public impact, pre-action oversight, and irreversible action. The full machine-readable determination appears in [`assessment.json`](assessment.json).

The public record supports authority and the eventual lack of protective effect. Comprehension, feasibility, and exercise remain indeterminate. This state preserves missing evidence as missing evidence instead of converting absence into a finding about operator behavior.

## Supported proposition

Public evidence supports the sequence from false track classification through correlated reports, a human launch order, engagement, and loss. It does not establish why the aircraft was misclassified, whether the reports were operationally independent, or which interventions were feasible before launch.

## Excluded conclusions

- The packet does not establish the precise technical or human cause of the misclassification.
- The packet does not establish the mental state or culpability of any individual operator or commander.
- The assessment does not determine legal responsibility or compliance with rules of engagement.
